How we handle numbers

Every environmental figure HumanityU displays comes from one of two places, and we keep them strictly separate.

Published measurements — figures about the state of the planet, taken from named scientific and governmental sources. We paraphrase them in our own words, name the publisher, and show the year the measurement refers to. We do not reproduce source text.

Your own entries — the quantities you type into a calculator. These are self-reported estimates, converted using published factors listed below. They are labelled as self-reported wherever they appear, they are never presented as measured, and they are excluded from any public total.

Where we could not establish a source to the standard we set ourselves, our intention is to remove the figure rather than present it confidently. This page lists what we have and, just as importantly, what we do not.

About the electricity factor. HumanityU applies a single United States figure — 384 grams of CO₂ per kWh, from Ember's Global Electricity Review — to every user, worldwide. Grid carbon intensity varies enormously: the global average is around 473 g, so for much of the world this understates emissions, while for a grid running largely on nuclear or hydro it overstates them. Within the United States alone, regional rates range from roughly 198 to 636 g. We are working on region-specific factors. Until then, treat any electricity figure as a rough order of magnitude, not a measurement of your household.

What we do not claim

  • Nothing in the app is verified, measured, audited or certified. Every quantity is one you typed in yourself.
  • We make no offsetting, neutrality or compensation claim. No carbon credit is ever bought, retired or held on your behalf.
  • We claim no environmental performance for the app or for your use of it, and we hold no sustainability certification.
  • Naming a source does not imply endorsement. None of the organisations listed here is affiliated with HumanityU or has reviewed it.
