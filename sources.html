How we handle numbers

Environmental figures in HumanityU are of two kinds, and we keep them separate. Not every figure in the app has a source listed here: some are HumanityU's own rough estimates.

Published measurements — figures about the state of the planet, from scientific and governmental sources. We paraphrase them in our own words and do not reproduce source text. The main ones are listed below with the publisher and the year. The short figures on the Crisis Map's zones do not name a source in the app yet, and they are not listed on this page.

Your own entries — the quantities you type into a calculator. These are self-reported estimates, converted using the factors listed below: some come from published sources, and others are HumanityU's own rough estimates. They are labeled as self-reported wherever they appear, they are never presented as measured, and they are excluded from any public total.

Where we could not establish a source to the standard we set ourselves, our intention is to remove the figure rather than present it confidently. This page lists what we have and, just as importantly, what we do not.

About the electricity factor. HumanityU applies a single United States average — 0.386 kg (386 grams) of CO₂ per kWh, which is 852.3 lb per MWh, the EPA eGRID national average used in the EPA Greenhouse Gas Equivalencies Calculator — to every user. Real grids range from near zero to over 800 grams per kWh, so for a grid running largely on coal this understates emissions, while for a grid running largely on nuclear, hydro, wind or solar it overstates them. Treat any electricity figure as a rough order of magnitude, not a measurement of your household.

What we do not claim

  • Nothing in the app is verified, measured, audited or certified. Every quantity is one you typed in yourself.
  • We make no offsetting, neutrality or compensation claim. No carbon credit is ever bought, retired or held on your behalf.
  • We claim no environmental performance for the app or for your use of it, and we hold no sustainability certification.
  • Naming a source does not imply endorsement. None of the organizations listed here is affiliated with HumanityU or has reviewed it.

Maps and geography

The land outline on the Crisis Map globe is derived from Natural Earth (1:110m data, as packaged by world-atlas), which is in the public domain. It is held inside the app and never downloaded. The globe loads no map tiles.

Corrections

If you believe a figure here is wrong, out of date, or attributed incorrectly, tell us at support@humanityu.app. We would rather correct a number than defend it.
